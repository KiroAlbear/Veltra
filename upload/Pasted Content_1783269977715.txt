What that CEO built is real, and the refinement you added — reviews tell you where the pain is; the founder decides how to heal it — is the part most people skip. That distinction alone is worth more than the scraping infrastructure. A few things worth sharpening before this becomes a permanent RASMY discipline, and then I'll show you exactly where it slots into what we're already building.

Where the model needs calibrating for Cliniva specifically
The generic version (G2, Capterra, Reddit, App Store) is built for Western SaaS, where buyers write reviews compulsively. Saudi dental clinic owners largely don't. If you point the scraper at G2/Capterra for "DentalKing" or "Medinous," you'll get thin, low-signal results — and worse, you'll unconsciously start optimising for a market that isn't actually yours.

Source	Signal quality for KSA dental	Why
G2 / Capterra	Low	Mostly Western SaaS buyers; KSA clinics rarely review software there
Google Maps reviews of the clinics themselves	High	Patients complain in Arabic about symptoms of bad software — "no one confirmed my appointment," "had to call three times," "no WhatsApp reminder" — that's your competitor's product failing, reported by the victim, not the buyer
Saudi Dental Society / Arabic Facebook & Telegram groups for clinic owners	Critical	This is where owners actually vent about DentalKing/Medinous billing and support — in Arabic, unfiltered, and invisible to anyone only scraping English-language review sites
App Store / Play Store (Arabic reviews of competitor patient apps)	Medium	Where it exists, it's specific and current
Reddit / G2	Low–Medium	Useful for Cliniva's eventual Canada market, not KSA Phase 1
/insights — the actual moat isn't "we scrape reviews." It's "we scrape the Arabic-language, patient-side, informal channels everyone else's English-language tooling can't see." That's a genuinely defensible edge, not a generic best practice.

The blind spot in the model itself
Two things to hold onto so this doesn't quietly become "build whatever the loudest reviewers want":

Survivorship/selection bias. People who write reviews are disproportionately furious or evangelical. The silent majority who mildly tolerated a product never shows up in the data. Treat "42% mentioned X" as directional, not as a vote count — track sample size alongside every percentage, or it'll get cited later as more rigorous than it is.
Reviews can't reveal what nobody's used yet. This method is excellent for closing competitive gaps and avoiding known failure modes — it cannot tell you about a feature category that doesn't exist. The Arabic AI Call Centre differentiator, for instance, wouldn't have come from mining DentalKing reviews; nobody complains about the absence of something they've never seen offered. Review mining sharpens execution; it doesn't replace founder-led bets on what doesn't exist yet.
The practical snag before you build the scraper
G2, Capterra, LinkedIn, and Glassdoor explicitly prohibit automated scraping in their terms of service, and Google has specific, narrower rules for Maps/review data. The durable version of this pipeline uses official review-export APIs or licensed aggregator data (or, for the Arabic Facebook/Telegram groups, manual or semi-manual reading at first) — not a custom scraper that works beautifully for three weeks and then gets IP-banned or triggers a legal notice right as it starts paying off.
